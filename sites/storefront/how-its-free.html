How FreeAppStore is free

No ads, no trials, no subscriptions, and no catch. Here is why it works.

The short answer

Web apps cost far less to build and run than native apps, and the platform's running costs are covered by its sister store, ProAppStore. Nobody has to make that money back from you, so FreeAppStore apps have no ads, no paid downloads, no trials, and no subscriptions.

Why web apps are cheaper

A native app usually means separate builds for iPhone and Android, developer program fees, store review for every release, and a share of every sale going to the store. Those costs are a big part of why so many apps fill up with ads or end their free trial after seven days.

A web app is one codebase that runs in any modern browser on phones, tablets, and computers. It ships the moment it is pushed, with no store review, and is shared with a link. AI coding tools make building one cheaper again. Read more in web apps vs native apps.

Who pays for the platform

FreeAppStore's hosting, sign-in, storage, realtime rooms, email, and the rest of the SDK backend run on shared infrastructure. Its costs are carried by ProAppStore, the paid platform in the same family, which earns its revenue from Pro apps listed on a subscription basis. Free apps never pay for that, and neither do their users.

What you never pay for

  • Downloading or opening an app
  • Removing ads: there are none
  • Unlocking features after a trial, or a monthly plan
  • Publishing an app: hosting, a subdomain, a GitHub repository, deployments, and the SDK backend are free for creators

FreeAppStore apps can't take payments: the publishing check rejects checkout and payment SDKs, and app hosting blocks the browser's Payment Request API. Ads are banned by our Terms, and apps are checked for ad network code.

What can cost money

Some apps let you connect an outside service, such as an AI provider, with your own API key. That provider bills you under your account, and the app should explain this before you connect. Building an app with AI in VibeCode uses your own AI provider connection in the same way. The platform's shared services also have fair-use limits.

Want to sell an app?

Paid apps belong on ProAppStore, a separate platform with its own SDK and subscription terms. FreeAppStore stays free.
