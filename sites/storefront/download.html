What it does

  • AI in your apps: apps that turn on Allow AI access offer tools (for example, the spreadsheet lets an AI read and edit your workbook). An AI app on your computer, such as Claude Code or Claude Desktop, uses those tools through the desktop app.
  • Local connection: the desktop app talks to AI apps on this computer only (127.0.0.1), protected by a key only you have. FreeAppStore's servers never see these requests and keep no copy of your app data. Your AI app does receive what the tools return; if it uses a cloud model, it sends that to its provider.
  • Apps in their own windows: open any FreeAppStore app from the desktop app's Store, or keep using it in your browser. Desktop windows and your browser keep separate copies of app data.
  • Contribute (optional): connect this computer to your account and it can make narration for store apps that ask for it, with a voice model that runs on your computer. Nothing is generated until you choose apps and download the model (350 MB). What it sends and who sees it.

First launch

These preview builds aren't signed by Apple or Microsoft yet, so your computer warns you the first time. That's expected.

Mac

  1. Open the .dmg and drag FreeAppStore to Applications.
  2. Open FreeAppStore from Applications. macOS blocks it the first time; click Done (or OK).
  3. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to the FreeAppStore message. Confirm with your password.

If macOS says the app "is damaged and can't be opened", run this once in Terminal, then open it again:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/FreeAppStore.app

Windows

  1. Run FreeAppStore-win-x64.exe.
  2. If Windows SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.

Linux

Make the AppImage executable, then run it:

chmod +x FreeAppStore-linux-x86_64.AppImage
./FreeAppStore-linux-x86_64.AppImage

Connect Claude Code or Claude Desktop

  1. Open the desktop app. On its Home screen, under Connect your AI, click Connect next to Claude Code or Claude Desktop. No terminal needed.
  2. Start a new Claude Code session, or quit and reopen Claude Desktop.
  3. Open an app (from the desktop app's Store, or in your browser with Allow AI access on), then ask Claude, for example, "list my open FreeAppStore apps".

FreeAppStore keeps running in the background from the menu bar (the system tray on Windows) after you close its window, and opens at login; you can turn that off on the Home screen. Pause AI access, on the Home screen or in the menu-bar menu, stops every AI call until you resume. To quit, choose Quit FreeAppStore from the menu-bar menu. Another AI app? Use Manual setup at the bottom of the Home screen.

About this preview

This is an early build for trying things out. On Windows and Linux it updates itself. On Mac it tells you when a new version is out and opens its download, because the Mac build isn't signed yet. Signed installers are coming. Your version is on the Account page. Found a problem? Open an issue with that version.
